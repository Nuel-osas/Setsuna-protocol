# Earn differentiation review

**Author: Codex · 2 October 2026.** Bounded review of the named competitors, official documentation, and selected Monad deployments. It is not a census of every vault or a security audit. Exact contract observations below use Monad block `109894238`; website descriptions and Euler's metadata were retrieved on the review date and may change independently.

**Verdict:** aggregation across lending protocols already exists on Monad. Automation, onchain limits, receipt shares, and liquidity-dependent instant withdrawals overlap with existing products; permissionless reallocation also exists. A close published Monad proposal uses internally validated public rebalancing and inline protocol rates. Setsuna now has a tested implementation of its particular allocation rule, but neither this review nor that implementation establishes a “first,” “only,” or “others cannot” claim.

This reinforces that putting yield and accessible funds in one interface is an existing product direction. This review did not validate aggregate competitor TVL or the statement that these named products collectively hold hundreds of millions on Monad.

## What Setsuna can credibly demonstrate

The narrower implemented design is a fixed pair of lending adapters, a deterministic rate-based target computed by the vault, permissionless execution, explicit cash availability, and restricted guardian powers. Its public executor cannot submit weights, APR values, destination addresses, or swap calldata. [Implementation and fork evidence](../EARN.md) establish those behaviors for the tested prototype. Destination risk approval and public deployment remain open. Caps alone do not mean the target-selection rule is enforced onchain: a curator can still make discretionary choices within caps.

This remains a **product hypothesis**, not an established unique category. Vaultus's plan also describes internally chosen moves; the idea of removing a discretionary keeper is not exclusively ours. Setsuna's actual parameters, adapter accounting, failure behavior, and reproducible evidence are what this build can demonstrate. Whether those differences matter to depositors needs user evidence. Do not equate more destinations with higher yield or independent risk.

Suggested wording while building:

> We're building automated yield vaults on Monad, with allocation rules enforced onchain and clear visibility into where funds are deployed and how much is available to withdraw.

Describe Aave/Morpho integration as a fork-tested prototype. The default factory's size gate excludes the selected Morpho market at the pinned block; the two-protocol test uses an explicit lower-threshold profile. Do not promise “withdraw anytime”: available cash can change, a buffer is shared across depositors, and the contract requires healthy valuation before redemption. [Implementation settings and limitations](../EARN.md).
